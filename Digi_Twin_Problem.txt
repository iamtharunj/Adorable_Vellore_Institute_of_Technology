All that you need to know about Digital Twin Challenge 2026
The future of medicine is proactive, not reactive. At the upcoming Happiest Health Reimagining and Reforming Healthcare in India Summit - 2026, Bengaluru, we are exploring the frontiers of digital health, focusing heavily on the concept of the Digital Twin. A Digital Twin in healthcare is a dynamic, virtual replica of a patient, constantly updated with real-time data from wearables, paired with Electronic Health Records (EHR). By simulating a patient’s unique physiology, it can predict adverse health events before they happen and personalize treatment protocols.

We are inviting the brightest minds from India's top university incubators to build the foundational prototypes of this future. We challenge student teams to build a proof-of-concept (PoC) Digital Twin Model focused on predicting a specific, localized health outcome.

Because building a complete, whole-body Digital Twin is beyond the scope of a short-term project, teams must select one specific chronic or lifestyle condition prevalent in India (e.g., Type 2 Diabetes, Hypertension, or Cardiovascular risk).

Eligibility

Open to students interested in AI, healthcare, data science and digital health, including students from relevant technical, medical and interdisciplinary backgrounds.

Phases of the Challenge:

Phase 1 - Prototype & Code Submission

The teams build their Digital Twin proof-of-concept and submit the completed project with required documentation as specified in the submission guidelines below under GitHub repository. 
Submitted solutions are evaluated by the technical and expert panel. Top 5 teams will be finalized by the jury, and an email communication will be sent to top 5 shortlisted team by Happiest Health 
The prototype must successfully demonstrate the fusion of two data streams:

Static/Historical data: Simulated Electronic Health Records (demographics, past diagnoses, lab results, genetic markers).
Dynamic/Real-time data: Simulated or open-source time-series data from consumer wearables/IoT sensors (heart rate variability, continuous glucose monitors, sleep stages, step count).
The output: A working algorithmic model that ingests these two streams and successfully predicts an adverse event (e.g., predicting a sudden glucose spike 2 hours in advance, or flagging a high-risk cardiovascular anomaly based on a week of sleep and heart-rate data). Teams must also present a conceptual UI dashboard showing how a doctor would interact with this virtual patient.

Navigating data constraints (The sandbox rules)

We recognize that access to real patient data is restricted by privacy laws like the DPDP Act and HIPAA. Teams are strictly required to use anonymized, open-source, or synthetic datasets for training their models.

For EHRs: Teams may use synthetic patient generation tools (like Synthea) or anonymized clinical databases (like MIMIC-IV).
For sensor data: Teams may utilize open-source wearable datasets or generate their own synthetic time-series data mimicking Apple Health, Google Fit, or dedicated medical IoT devices.
